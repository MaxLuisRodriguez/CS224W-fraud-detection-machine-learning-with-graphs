# Investigate Together

Joint evidence acquisition for related fraud alerts — CS224W research project.

**Research question:** Can jointly choosing evidence requests across related fraud alerts improve money-laundering detection under a fixed investigation budget, beyond investigating alerts individually with the same shared cache?

A request for one account's history can inform several alerts, and its value may emerge only after follow-up requests. The research tests whether a learned joint policy improves on cached local policies, global greedy acquisition, and nonmyopic planning.

## Status

The latest literature audit demotes the dense continuation-value model to a baseline. The conditional research direction is adaptive computation of action–case influence, compared against efficient scalar joint models and established acquisition methods. Novelty and experimental advantage remain unproven.

Plans and downloaded IBM HI-Small data only. There are no trained models, validated environment tests, or measured acquisition gains. The September 25 audit checked the local CSV header and identified its duplicate Account column names; this is not a completed dataset audit.

Implementation will begin from scratch. The earlier unfinished scaffold remains outside the repository in the sibling folder CS224W-fraud-detection-machine-learning-with-graphs-scaffold-archive-20260923. Older plans are retained under docs/history and in dated snapshots; they do not override the active plan.

No cloud resources have been launched or spending incurred by this planning revision. Raw data, environments, and experiment artifacts remain excluded from Git. Labels and evaluator-only annotations must never enter investigation-tool observations.
